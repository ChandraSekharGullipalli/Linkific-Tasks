# Day 19 – Research Methodology & Data Research

## Project: Student Academic Performance Analysis

### 1. Project Overview

This project demonstrates the application of research methodology and statistical data analysis to understand student academic performance.

The research focuses on identifying relationships between study hours, attendance percentage, and examination scores using quantitative research methods.

The project covers the complete research framework, including problem identification, research question formulation, hypothesis development, data collection planning, statistical analysis, and research documentation.

### 4. Research Problem

Students demonstrate different levels of academic performance due to various factors.

This research investigates whether study hours and attendance percentage are associated with examination performance.

### 5. Research Questions

1. Is daily study time associated with examination scores?
2. Is attendance percentage associated with academic performance?
3. Which factors show stronger relationships with examination results?
4. How can statistical analysis support educational research?

### 6. Research Hypotheses

**Hypothesis 1: Study Hours and Examination Scores**

- H0: There is no statistically significant linear relationship between study hours and examination scores.
- H1: There is a statistically significant linear relationship between study hours and examination scores.

**Hypothesis 2: Attendance and Examination Scores**

- H0: There is no statistically significant linear relationship between attendance and examination scores.
- H1: There is a statistically significant linear relationship between attendance and examination scores.

### 9. Data Analysis Techniques

**Descriptive Statistics**
- Mean
- Median
- Mode
- Minimum
- Maximum
- Standard Deviation

**Exploratory Data Analysis**
- Histograms
- Bar Charts
- Scatter Plots
- Box Plots
- Correlation Heatmaps

**Correlation Analysis**

Pearson correlation is used to examine linear relationships between numerical variables.

**Hypothesis Testing**

Pearson correlation significance tests are used to evaluate the research hypotheses.

**Statistical Significance**

The significance level is set at 0.05.

- p-value < 0.05: Reject the null hypothesis.
- p-value >= 0.05: Fail to reject the null hypothesis.

**Effect Size**

Pearson's correlation coefficient is reported to describe the strength and direction of the relationship.

### 10. Sample Analysis Results

The project uses an illustrative dataset containing 10 student records.

| Analysis | Sample Result |
|---|---:|
| Average Study Hours | 3.5 |
| Average Attendance | 77.4% |
| Average Exam Score | 62.4 |
| Study Hours vs Exam Score | r = 0.987 |
| Attendance vs Exam Score | r = 0.993 |

Both variables demonstrate very strong positive linear associations in the illustrative sample.

The sample hypothesis tests produce statistically significant results at the 0.05 level.

**Important:** These results are based on artificially generated sample data and must not be presented as actual survey findings.

### 12. Research Ethics

- Obtain informed consent from participants.
- Maintain participant confidentiality.
- Avoid unnecessary personal data collection.
- Store research data securely.
- Use data only for educational research.
- Report findings honestly.
- Avoid unsupported conclusions.

### 13. Research Limitations

- The illustrative dataset contains only 10 records.
- Sample results cannot be generalized to all students.
- Self-reported data may contain inaccuracies.
- Other academic performance factors are not included.
- Correlation does not establish causation.
